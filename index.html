"看"与"被看"的 simultaneity（同时性），对《青春性事与死亡》所呈现的那种具身性欲而言至关重要。在营地的放映室里，克里丝与比利看着初代《瘴气营地》的片段，其中包括比利角色与另一名辅导员做爱的段落。虽没有说出"不"，比利的 visibly uncomfortable（明显不适）。但当小死亡开始逼近，比利的脸从不适与顺从，转向某种更好奇的神情。我们在她呻吟的俯拍与一个小死亡攀爬楼梯、窥看床上她身体的 POV 之间切换。它们靠近，头映在她的眼白里，向下刺去。"我同时是两者，"比利后来解释——既是杀手也是受害者，既是阴蒂性高潮也是阳具性高潮，既是观看者也是被看者。克里丝为"你眼中的那个神情"所迷，那神情同时显露痛楚、欢愉、震惊、恐怖与顿悟。见到此景，克里丝意识到自己再也无法停留在学术的疏离中："我不只想看着它、把它放进一部电影，我也想亲身体验，"她对比利说，"你愿意帮我吗？"

克里丝的性幻想，是成为"最终女孩"，乳房弹跳着、徒劳地从一大一小死亡面前奔逃，最终被刺穿。克里丝描述的是：渴望被渴望到极致暴力的程度，渴望自己诱人至极、被追逐到违背己愿，最终被强奸、被谋杀。要进入这幻想，她必须让性淹没自己的感官。她那种自我批判的思维，必须被吞没一切的快感摧毁，好让这些欲望——违拗于她的政治立场——附着于青春期吸纳入体的、那些压迫性的、决定性的叙事，并由此形成某种创伤性的自我感。当她最终直面自己的幻想、与小死亡肉身相对时，她的眼镜坠落、碎裂。她自我觉知的工具碎了，而她一头扎进那场双方合意的、非合意的扮演，为活命而奔逃。

克里丝用以接纳自身幻想、达成性互的主体性的开场白，是"我在想关于转变 (transforming)"——这绝非巧合。尽管《瘴气营地》显然关乎暴力与欲望的关系，它更尖锐地，是关于跨性别之后 (after transitioning) 的跨性别性欲。裘德·多伊尔主张，必须把克里丝与比利理解为跨性别女性，因为《瘴气营地》从根本上、彻底地"关乎学习以一名拥有阴茎的酷儿女性的身份去驾驭性"。他写道：

"[比利] 已学会把那根长矛作为自己性体验的一部分来接纳、甚至欢迎……并教会 [克里丝] 如何让长矛在自己的性生活里扮演一个角色，而不引发羞耻的漩涡，这很甜。为了熬过性，她必须解离，具体而言，她必须想象自己是恐怖片里那个女孩——被那个用象征性阳具消灭女性的邪恶跨性别者杀死的女孩。想成为任何一个女孩，更别提是这个女孩，都感觉'厌女'而羞耻，于是她 shut down（封闭自己）。"

片中《瘴气营地》系列的导演名叫 Ray Blanchard，取自那位性学家——他的"跨性别分类学"把跨性别女性归类为要么是"同性恋跨性别者"（以女性化姿态吸引男性），要么是"自我异性恋爱好型跨性别者"（因拥有一个女性身体与性器的念头而兴奋）。布兰查德的分类未将顺性别女性纳入；而当此类研究真的纳入她们时，它们表明大多数女性——无论顺性别或跨性别——在性中都体验过对身体与性器的具身唤起，因而所有人都有某种程度的自我异性恋爱好。尽管如此，布兰查德的研究仍对北美跨性别医疗产生了不成比例的物质性影响，助长了把性别不安与跨性别体验持续病理化的倾向，以及那种对跨性别者、尤其是跨性别女性"性偏差"的残酷规训文化。

克里丝的羞耻根植于这份遗产：她害怕自己"作为一个女性而被渴望"、以自我异性恋爱好方式看待自己，是一种剥削。克里丝钻进脑子里，是为了逃离身体。当你的身体并非你自己，有时脑子是个更舒服的所在。那是一种对"因渴望被一个把自己视为女性身体的伴侣享受"而遭受病理化压力的创伤性反应。把欲望劈成被投射的观看者，是一种放弃能动性与权力的努力，也是否认那追逐之力中男性成分的努力。克里丝对自己性欲那种自我贬抑的态度，与否认、对成为掠夺者的恐惧，以及对自己身体情欲潜能的疏离缠在一起。她几乎含泪地表达自己"不善做爱"。

这种"被追逐的快感及其随之而来的羞耻"，在《青春性事与死亡》主创团队之外、申布伦本人的前作里也有描述。吉莲·安德森的书《Want》（2024）虽以安德森之名出版，实则是女性匿名投稿、细述自己性幻想的合集（尽管据称其中一篇出自安德森本人）。许多投稿直面性中的权力与暴力，好几篇描摹被压倒、被制服、被攻击、被强奸的梦。即便匿名，这些女性仍费力地解释：她们是女权主义者，现实里并不希望如此，而幻想中的追逐者得到了她们的同意。正如评论者 Marcela Santos Brigida 所辨认出的，她们的"内在审查"始终在场。这种高度警觉，与克里丝害怕显得"#MeToo 式"的焦虑是相似的感觉。这些女性为自己被视为"不仅会容许这暴力发生、还会从中取乐"的那种人而感到羞耻。安德森把这些欲望从内在的羞耻，护送进公开的展演，恰好呼应了她为克里丝所起的引领作用。

申布伦此前的影片，同样受到互联网对青少年性欲之影响。当《荧屏在发光》上映，格雷格·阿拉基为《Filmmaker Magazine》采访申布伦，申布伦谈到如何通过书写自己接下来的作品（即《青春性事与死亡》）来处理自己的性欲。" transition 之前，我并非无性恋——我欲望充沛——但在错误的身体里拥有好的性，是不可能的，"他们解释，"可得的，唯有彻底的解离。" 互联网上发生在孩子与青少年身上的"带着性暗示的怪事"，被视为心理上的塑形、偶尔的阻滞，且坦率地说"怪"（用申布伦自己的话）。在《荧屏在发光》里，更自信的 Maddy 问羞怯内向的 Owen："你喜欢男生？喜欢女生？"他怯懦地答："我想我喜欢电视节目。"

虚构即角色扮演，而在申布伦的电影里，它对那些真实被现实否认了的人们的性表达而言，至关重要。《瘴气营地》系列被揭示为小死亡的造物——它从湖底的洞里出现，并在真实发生的电影中，充当一个超越性的编导演合一者。小死亡开启了一个游戏的安全空间，其中暴力的限度与性的限度既非常真实，又流动而顽固。这让小死亡得以探索、并沉醉于一种曾被营地客们（在它少年时将其处决）剥夺的权力位置。对克里丝而言，她能向一个既是陌生人、又极亲近的人敞开自己的性欲——考虑到克里丝通过《瘴气营地》电影对比利长期的准社会关系式痴迷。于是，《瘴气营地》影片的互文指涉，发展出一套性欲的架构。每一部都是一部迭代式的色情作品，调用同样的场景、角色与桥段，服务于小死亡的满足。因而比利把《瘴气营地》的各部分称为"他的电影"。

电影的功能就是被看。通过这些游戏创制出这些人造物、这些《瘴气营地》影片，坚持把"被认出"作为性与性权力的一个本质成分。小死亡的头是一个空调通风口，表达着：通过公开指认"跨性别杀手"这一桥段，来释放、来厘清空气。那同心方框也可能唤起"屏幕中的屏幕"，以及"看与被看之看的循环"。在小死亡那段壮丽的、一镜到底的杀戮狂潮里，一次杀戮之后，它停下休憩。头中央那块不透明的方块散去雾气，我们看见它的眼睛向外凝视。正如比利的眼睛在她初次认出自己既是观看者也是被看者时所显现，此刻，小死亡看见了我们。

《青春性事与死亡》的批评者，批评它过度用力且无休止地使用视觉隐喻、不断使眼色，以及内置的智识防御姿态。为《Salon》撰稿的科尔曼·斯皮尔德痛斥它把意识形态权威下放给观众，忽视了"所有这些点子核心处的那部电影"。然而，这并非退却或叛逃到智识主义，而是一种正成为申布伦 signature（标志）的、电影制作模式的相互作用。"电影是这么美的一种媒介，而我们主要拿它当叙事投递机制来用，是对其力量的一种有限利用，"导演在《i-D》杂志的采访中说，"你可以让它复杂化、质询、颠覆、挑逗、挫败。" 如果整条线索似乎更执着于对经典恐怖片里生殖器寓言的象征化处理，而牺牲了人物塑造，那么，嗯，大概确实如此。

《青春性事与死亡》通过一则暴力却乐观的自我接纳故事，欢迎学术的、色情的、感官的与叙事的多元读法。影片鼓励我们沉溺于反常 (perversion) 的快感，让我们得以同时占据克里丝、比利与小死亡彼此叠合、相互确证的欲望与视点。形式、视点与欲望的叠加，在最终高潮里被视觉化：小死亡刺穿比利、再刺入克里丝，三人被死亡与狂喜、一根生锈长矛的贯穿， ecstatic（狂喜）地缚在一起。一篇现已删除的博客，把这瞬间比作手术式的 transition：走上手术台，醒来时已变得更好。两位女性在血泊的余韵中醒来，克里丝得以重返"真实"世界，未穿胸衣、未戴眼镜，终于在身心之中安适。当小死亡的幽灵再次升起，克里丝望向摄影机之外，露出欢迎的微笑。

When your relationship to sex, especially in your formative years, is framed by movies, television, and the internet, it is near impossible to escape the feeling that sex is something performed and viewed, with these two parts of the equation being significantly and functionally removed from each other. Sex is displayed and offered, watched and received. To engage, then, in the sex act, you grapple with the presence of the screen: what separates you from a partner, the film between performance and pleasure.

Jane Schoenbrun's Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma is wholeheartedly self-aware; it does what it says on the tin. Front-loading its profound hyperreferentiality to the legacy of slasher films affords Schoenbrun the novel opportunity to probe the increasingly reflexive and iterative nature of desire and fantasy in the screen age. The two-way mirror of the internet is an opaque multiplier of the phenomena well described by John Berger that 'A woman must continually watch herself.' Schoenbrun leaps forward from this to question how queer注酷儿，泛指非异性恋规范的身份与欲望；本片以酷儿视角重读类型片。 women and trans queer women position themselves in cycles of looking and being looked at, as well as the desires this arouses, and the shame this desire induces. Teenage Sex and Death is a whirlwind of analysis put to drama and fountains of blood, exploring monstrous transness, self-policed "autogynephilia," and consensual non-consent kink-play. Literality becomes a tool of inoculation: Little Death注片中杀手名，源自法文「la petite mort」(字面「小死亡」)，是性高潮的委婉说法，呼应片名主题。, the masked killer haunting the film-within-the-film, is an orgasm (la petite mort), the thrusting spear is penetration/a penis, the mountains of junk food are surrogate satisfaction. By wasting no time with the obvious, the film can more directly address this cultural inheritance, peeling back mutually assured constructions of fantasy, desire, and queerness, layer by layer. The camera allows Schoenbrun to frame this through looking: the politics of seeing, and the sexuality of being seen, superimposed.

Teenage Sex and Death follows Kris (Hannah Einbinder), a young filmmaker tasked with resurrecting the 'zombie IP' of the Camp Miasma slasher franchise, deemed too profitable to ignore but too problematic for anything short of a queer – in Kris's agent's words "woke" – modern reappraisal. Kris makes her pilgrimage to Camp Tivoli, the original shooting location of the first instalment of Camp Miasma, and now residence of the actress who played its Final Girl, Billy Presley (Gillian Anderson). The two women share an obsession with the series, drawn by something unspeakable in the confluence of blood and lust within Camp Miasma, and while Kris initially tries to channel this force into her adaption, Billy helps her unleash something far more transformative.

The first time Kris tries to have sex with Billy, she prepares herself by smoking a joint in the bathroom. As she stares in the mirror, a portrait of a pale young woman with gouged eyes and a mouth gaping and smeared with blood flashes in the reflection. In the legend of Bloody Mary, if summoned by a woman the spirit would show either the face of their future husband, or a skull if they were destined to die before marriage. Kris tries to shake off this foreboding sign, removing her glasses. She emerges from the bathroom, sheepishly baring her breasts but hiding her face behind her hair, pantomiming feminine allure. In each screening I've been in, this moment draws a laugh from the audience. She is shy, attempting an idea of brazen sexuality, overwhelming self-conscious to the point of ridicule. In a word, adolescent. She and Billy begin having sex, Billy asking her how she wants to be touched, but Kris brushes it off, awkwardly repeating "dealer's choice" with forced breathiness. After a few tense moments, she clams up, relenting "I can't do this." The camera drops to the ground with a thud.

We return to the women lying by the fire on a bed of gummy candy wrappers. Though still half naked, something in Kris' defensiveness has slipped, and she can speak frankly about her felt distance from her own sexuality. Her glasses are back on. Kris, seemingly for the first time, broaches her sexual fantasies, where she is "a victim, a slut," hunted by the killer Little Death on the grounds of Camp Miasma. Kris' glasses are remarkably similar to Midge's in Alfred Hitchcock's Vertigo: thick, black, and full-rimmed. Erika Balsom described the spectacles as indicative of "a woman who knows too much." Kris' anxieties about being a cliche woman in distress, wanting the vulnerability often forced upon women, her fears of being misogynistic in her own desires, obstruct her ability to enjoy herself sexually. But Balsom also describes the bespectacled woman as moving "towards being an active subject of vision and knowledge," and as Kris opens up about her desires she allows herself real comfort and arousal.

The simultaneity of seeing and being seen is essential to the embodied sexuality Teenage Sex and Death represents. In the camp projection room, Kris and Billy watch scenes from the original Camp Miasma, including the part where Billy's character has sex with another counsellor. While there isn't a spoken "no," Billy is visibly uncomfortable. But Billy's face shifts from discomfort and resignation to something more curious when Little Death begins to approach. We cut between an overhead shot of her moans with a creeping POV of Little Death climbing the stairs, seeing her body on the bed. They approach, their head reflected in her sclera, and stab downwards. "I was both of us," Billy later explains, both killer and victim, both clitoral and phallic orgasm, both watcher and watched. Kris is fascinated by "that look in your eye," revealing at once pain, pleasure, shock, horror, realisation. Seeing this, Kris realises she can no longer live at an academic remove: "I don't just want to watch it and put it in a movie, I want to experience it too," she tells Billy, "Will you help me?"

Kris' sexual fantasy is to be the final girl, tits bouncing as she runs in vain from Little Death before being finally penetrated. Kris describes wanting to be wanted to the point of extreme violence, to be so desirable as to be pursued beyond her will, finally raped and murdered. To enter the fantasy she must let sex overwhelm her senses. Her self-critical thinking must be obliterated by all-consuming pleasure, allowing these desires to adhere, against her politics, to oppressive and definitive narratives imbibed in adolescence that have formed a traumatic sense of self. When she is finally confronted by her fantasy, facing Little Death in the flesh, her glasses fall and break. Her tool of self-awareness breaks as she enters the play of consensual non-consent and she runs for her life.

It is no coincidence that Kris' inaugurating words into accepting her fantasies and achieving sexual mutuality are "I think about transforming." While Camp Miasma is clearly about the relationship between violence and desire, it is more pointedly about trans sexuality after transitioning. Jude Doyle argues it is essential to understand Kris and Billy as trans women, as Camp Miasma is fundamentally and totally "about learning to navigate sex as a queer woman with a penis." He writes: "[Billy] has learned to include, and even welcome, the spear as part of her sexual experience, and … teaches [Kris] how to allow the spear to play a role in her sex life without causing a shame spiral, which is very sweet. In order to get through sex, she has to dissociate, and specifically, she has to imagine she's the girl in the horror movie—the one who gets killed by the evil trans person who obliterates women with his symbolic penis. Wanting to be any girl, let alone this girl, feels 'misogynistic' and shameful, so she shuts down."

The in-universe director of the Camp Miasma film series is named Ray Blanchard, after the sexologist whose "transsexualism typology" categorises of trans women as either: "homosexual transsexuals," presenting feminine to attract men; or "autogynephilic transsexuals," who are turned on at the idea of having a female body and genitals. Blanchard failed to account for cis women in his categorisation, and when such studies are done they demonstrate most women, cis or trans, experience embodied arousal at their body and genitals during sex and therefore all have some level of autogynephilia. Nevertheless, Blanchard's work had outsized material impact on North American trans health care, contributing to the persistent pathologisation of gender dysphoria and trans experiences, and to the cruel culture of policing "sexual deviancy" in trans people, particularly trans women.

Kris' shame is rooted in this inheritance: she is afraid that wanting to be desired as a woman, viewing herself in an autogynephilic way, is exploitative. Kris gets in her head to get out of her body. When your body isn't who you are, sometimes your head is a nicer place to be. It is a traumatic response to pressure of being pathologised for wanting to be enjoyed by a partner who sees them as having a female body. The splitting of desire into the projected viewer is an effort to relinquish agency and power, as well as deny the masculine component of the pursuing force. Kris' self-effacing approach to her own sexuality is tied up in denial, fears of being predatory, and a disconnection from the erotic potential of her own body. She expresses this, almost in tears, at being "bad at sex."

This pleasure in being pursued and the ensuing shame is described in previous works from the Teenage Sex and Death team outside Schoenbrun themselves. Gillian Anderson's book Want (2024), while published under Anderson's name, is a collection of anonymous submissions from women detailing their sexual fantasies (though reportedly one entry is by Anderson herself). Many of the entries confront power and violence in sex, with several describing dreams of being overpowered, subdued, assaulted, and raped. Even through their anonymity, these women take pains to explain they are feminists, they don't wish for this in real life, and that the pursuer in their fantasy has their consent. Their "internal censure," as reviewer Marcela Santos Brigida identifies it, "is very much present." This hyper-vigilance describes a similar feeling to Kris's anxieties about seeming "me-too-y". These women are ashamed of being seen as the kind of person who would not only allow this violence to happen, but also to derive pleasure from it. Anderson chaperones these desires through insular shame into public display, complimenting her initiating role for Kris.

Schoenbrun's previous films have also been informed by the influence of the internet on teenage sexuality. When I Saw the TV Glow was released, Gregg Araki interviewed Schoenbrun for Filmmaker Magazine, where they described processing their sexuality through writing their upcoming work (what would be Teenage Sex and Death). "Pre-transition, it wasn't that I was asexual – I had plenty of desire – but having good sex in the wrong body was impossible," they explained. "What was available was full dissociation." The "weird shit with sexual undertones" that happens to kids and teenagers on the internet is understood to be psychologically formative, occasionally stunting, and frankly "weird" (in Schoenbrun's own words). In TV Glow, the older, more confident Maddy asks the painfully introverted Owen, "Do you like boys? Do you like girls?" To which he meekly responds, "I think I like TV shows."

Fiction is roleplay, and in Schoenbrun's films, it is essential for the sexual expression of people whose truth has been denied by reality. The Camp Miasma films are revealed to be creations of Little Death, who emerges from the hole at the bottom of the lake and operates as a transcendent writer-director-actor in the very real films that take place. Little Death initiates a safe space of play where the limits of violence and sexuality are both very real as well as fluid and intransigent. This allows Little Death to explore and delight in a position of power that was denied to them by the campers' execution of them as a teenager. For Kris, she can open up about her sexuality to someone who is simultaneously a stranger as well as someone very close to them, given Kris' long term parasocial obsession with Billy via the Camp Miasma movies. The intratextual referencing of the Camp Miasma films, then, develops an architecture of sexual fantasy. Each film is a work of iterative pornography, deploying the same settings, characters, and tropes in service of Little Death's gratification. Hence Billy's description of the instalments of Camp Miasma "his movies".

The function of a film is to be watched. The creation of these artefacts, the Camp Miasma films, through the play, insists on recognition being an essential component of sex and sexual power. Little Death's head is an air conditioning vent, expressing the release and clearing the air by openly addressing the trope of the trans killer. The concentric squares may also evoke screens within screens, and the cycle of watching being watched watching. During Little Death's magnificent one-shot killing spree, there is a moment after a kill where they stop and rest. The opaque square in the centre of the head defogs, and we see their eyes staring out. As Billy's eyes showed the moment she first understands herself as the watcher and the watched, here Little Death sees us.

Teenage Sex and Death's detractors have criticised its heavy-handed and incessant use of visual metaphor, constant winking, and built-in intellectual defensiveness. Writing for Salon, Colman Spilde lambasted its relegation of ideological authority onto the audience, neglecting the "film at the core of all of these ideas." Rather than a retreat or defection to intellectualism, however, this is an interplay of filmmaking modes that is becoming Schoenbrun's signature. "Film is such a beautiful medium, and the fact that we primarily use it as a narrative delivery mechanism is such a limited use of its power," the director stated in an interview with i-D magazine: "You can complicate, and interrogate, and subvert, and titillate, and frustrate." If entire throughlines of the film seem more preoccupied with a symbolic treatment of genital allegories in classic horror films at the expense of characterisation, well, they probably are.

Teenage Sex and Death welcomes the plurality of academic, erotic, sensual, and narrative readings through a violent yet optimistic story of self-acceptance. The film encourages us to indulge in the pleasures of perversion, allowing us to simultaneously occupy the points of view and mutually affirming desires of Kris, Billy, and Little Death. The superposition of forms, perspectives, and desires is visualised in the final climax: Little Death stabs through Billy into Kris, the three held together through an ecstatic blend of death and delight and a skewering rusty spear. A now-deleted blog post likened this moment to surgical transitioning: going under the knife and waking up changed for the better. The women wake up in a blood-soaked afterglow, and Kris is able to re-enter the 'real' world, braless and bare-eyed, finally at ease in her body and mind. As the spectre of Little Death rises once again, Kris looks beyond the camera with a welcoming smile.

简·申布伦对 1980 年代糟粕恐怖片的新致敬，要观众这次换个方式：别再过度分析了。

恐怖电影那种越界的天性，赋予了讲故事的人探查禁忌的自由，尤其是关于性与死亡。有时这些议题被处理得审慎而富洞见。但更多时候，并非如此。性别不一致 (gender-nonconformity)，尤其常被表现为恶之根源。《沉默的羔羊》《亨利：连环杀手肖像》《德州电锯杀人狂》《Death Spa》《剃刀边缘》《In Dreams》《洛基恐怖秀》《睡眠营》，以及更近的《钛》，还有它们的老祖母《惊魂记》——这些电影里都有性别错乱的杀人狂。在互联网时代之前，跨性别孩子或许第一次在租来的录像带上看见一个像自己的人——然后，紧接着，看见自己作为待消灭的怪物。

简·申布伦的新作恐怖/喜剧/爱情片《瘴气营地的青春性事与死亡》，探索了酷儿恐怖迷与他们所处类型之间那种复杂的、受虐般的关系：这个类型既歌颂他们、又妖魔化他们，先赋予他们声音、再用砍刀把他们的头颅剁下。连同申布伦此前的《我们都要去世界的尽头》与《荧屏在发光》，《青春性事与死亡》构成了关于酷儿性、孤立，以及对恐怖媒介过度认同的"精神三部曲"的第三部分。在第一部里，一个孤独的、性别不一致的孩子迷失在对一则网络 creepypasta 的危险痴迷中。在《荧屏在发光》里，一个名为 Owen 的、深度抑郁的性别不一致角色，像幽灵般蹒跚度日，在一档诡异的 1990 年代儿童电视剧里寻找真实的自我。而在《青春性事与死亡》里，一个焦虑、被压抑的性别不一致艺术家，在砍杀片的血腥世界里找到了自我实现。

"天哪，她是个男孩！"

申布伦的"片中片"《瘴气营地》，指涉了许多为人钟爱的恐怖系列，而它在视觉上尤其模仿《十三号星期五》（还点缀了点《寂静岭》的金字塔头）。但它压倒性地、决绝地，是一封写给罗伯特·希尔兹克《睡眠营》的情书——那部 1980 年代的邪典注Cult，指拥有狂热小众粉丝群、常被主流忽视的作品或导演风格；申布伦常被归入邪典作者。砍杀片，讲一群粗鄙、发情的少男少女与辅导员，在一个管理混乱的夏令营里被杀死。这部低成本垃圾盛宴在申布伦的电影里被反复点名。那片营地与纽约州 Argyle 以南约一小时车程的哈德逊河谷小镇 Tivoli 同名，而《睡眠营》正是在那里拍摄。《瘴气营地》的起源故事，也与《睡眠营》的结尾平行。

《睡眠营》于"觉醒前"的 1983 年上映，它用《惊魂记》式的终极揭示——那个怯懦、长期被霸凌的儿童主角 Angela 是个 AMAB（出生指派男性）的连环杀手——大概意在制造恐同的惊吓效果。整部电影里，酷儿性带来腐化与死亡。Angela 父亲的同性关系导致混乱、可能的乱伦，以及某种致命的划船事故。Angela 的性别转换，是被她那个疯癫的阿姨（同时也是医生）强加于她的，方式非常像 J.K. 罗琳所想象中的性别肯定医疗。然而，糟糕的表演、诡异的台词、暴露的男装（1980 年代男辅导员制服显然由露脐上衣、热裤，以及偶尔的渔网肌肉衫组成），加上角色们对异性恋表演的那种青春期式痴迷，把这部电影变成了一场 camp（双关无法避免）的操练。

一些跨性别粉丝把 Angela 认作一位复仇式的反英雄。"Angela 干掉的那些人，每一个都 justification 得惊人，"曾短暂出镜于《荧屏在发光》的跨性别 YouTuber 兼音乐人 May Leitz 说，"她是个女王。" 此言不虚：Angela 杀掉的角色全都不是东西。其中两个是恋童癖——一个被弓箭射死，另一个被煮进一大锅玉米里。多数是在浴室、水潭这类政治意味浓重的空间里霸凌 Angela 的恶霸。还有一个自私的男友，越过了 Angela 的边界，继而出轨——往好了说是混蛋，往坏了说是 creep。当代观众，尤其跨观众，很容易为杀人的 Angela 喝彩。

这么做是否有问题？我们该为享受它而感到罪恶吗？如果你是跨性别者呢？如果你是跨性别者，但是"错的那种"跨——比如跨男或 agender（无性别），而非像 Angela 那样的跨女呢？这是一种 reclaim（重夺），还是某种背叛？

《瘴气营地的青春性事与死亡》里处在中心的两位主要角色，代表了应对此类棘手问题的两种不同方式。一位，是那场由在线酷儿媒体话语组成的"啄 party"的老兵，她把问题过度检视、过度分析、过度审查到自我 miserable（痛苦）。另一位，则满足于抽大麻、吃糖果，好好享受这该死的电一影。

瘴气 (Miasma)

在希腊神话里，miasma 是一种会沾染罪行的、具传染性的精神污染，可附着于一处地方，兴风作浪，直到它被清除。在申布伦的"片中片"《瘴气营地》里，那桩罪，是对一个性别流动的孩子施加的恐跨谋杀；而那诅咒，是一个叫"小死亡"的砍杀杀手，戴着通风口面具、穿着塑胶衣，从湖里爬出，用一根阳具般的长矛谋杀发情的异性恋少年。

《青春性事与死亡》的主角，是那个极度千禧一代的克里丝（汉娜·爱宾德饰），一位受命复活疲态尽显的《瘴气营地》系列的年轻独立导演，片厂下令要它"高级化"，即"觉醒"。克里丝从一开始就知道自己是个 diversity hire（多元雇用），被选中来给这个系列"洗白"其问题元素，因为她/他们是酷儿——一种涉及无聊桌游、以及与名叫 Thor 的蠢笨双性恋者组成多角关系的酷儿。

和许多被榨干的 IP 一样，新版《瘴气营地》打算拍一部聚焦于小死亡起源故事的前传。（换言之，这是一部必须以一个小酷儿孩子在一场仇恨犯罪中被溺死收场的电影。）但克里丝反而迷上了首部《瘴气营地》的女主角比利（吉莲·安德森以十足诺玛·戴斯蒙德模式饰演），后者已退出演艺圈，隐居在首作拍摄地的废弃夏令营。克里丝想让故事以比利为主角推进。在比利身上，克里丝看见了酷儿神经质者最宏大的幻想：一个富有、火辣的年长者，愿意把你身上不对劲的东西干出去。

但克里丝在性上有个心结。她聪明过了头。她借工作躲开性，把性理智化，在性中解离，唯有把自己想象成旧《瘴气营地》电影里的角色，才能体验任何快感。尽管满口酷儿女权修辞，克里丝幻想的，是成为那种被蒙面杀手（其名是"高潮"的法语婉语）追得半裸奔逃的傻妞。她知道这对她不好——性的垃圾食品，正如比利在他们第一餐时骄傲地用银盘端上的 KFC，带着蘸酱 sensuously（性感地）吞咽。

这部电影在这层意义上是自传性的。在《Filmmaker Magazine》的采访中，申布伦对格雷格·阿拉基说："transition 之前，我并非无性恋——我欲望充沛——但在错误的身体里拥有好的性，是不可能的。"

如此强烈地在性上认同一部垃圾砍杀片，是错的吗？把自己看作杀手与受害者之间那层半透明的屏幕，是厌女或自我毁灭吗？在两人私下的《瘴气营地》观影中，克里丝像抢答的老师宠儿，在另一名学生还举着手时便喊出答案，吱吱地说"这部分恐跨"，比利只简单回了一句"闭嘴"。"好，"克里丝说。

净化 (Katharsis)

要驱除 miasma，希腊人举行净化仪式。这些仪式可能包含对原初触犯者施加致命复仇。有时，祭司用水或祭牲之血洗去 miasma。

在《青春性事与死亡》里，一团过度智识焦虑的 miasma 笼罩着克里丝的心智，使她无法抵达性狂喜。为净化这诅咒，比利召唤小死亡，去砍穿那些体现克里丝心结的形象——那恼人的多角关系、那讨嫌的片厂高管、那唯利是图的经纪人，以及最终，克里丝最大的敌人：她自己——并以血为她施洗。"全都是 flesh and fluids（肉与体液）而已，"比利说，对抗着克里丝对砍杀类型性别动力无尽的开炮。"

幻想看着伴侣的伴侣被杀，只为让自己能高潮，这是有毒的吗？为了能上床而关掉自己的脑子，这是丧失力量吗？比利与克里丝之间的年龄差有问题吗？克里丝自私地征用比利、通过暴力来在性上治愈自己，这自私吗？这部电影似乎在挑衅我们，去在专栏文章、视频杂谈和社交媒体上无休止地拆解这些问题。难点在于：闭嘴。好吗？

随着 1980 年代转入 1990 年代，砍杀片开始后继乏力。你能杀的发情少年终究有限，观众迟早厌烦。但当这股潮流开始 stale，那些先前把该类型贬为低俗厌女垃圾的评论者，开始把它重估为"关于厌女、而非表达厌女"的评论。在 Carol J. Glover 1992 年的著作《男人、女人与电锯：现代恐怖片中的性别》中，她主张恐怖片其实是关于女性反抗压迫性男性凝视（在《万圣节》《黑色圣诞》等片中以杀手 POV 摄影机呈现），并从男性压迫者手中夺回能动性。正是这本书创造了"最终女孩"一词，指那个幸存大屠杀、击败坏蛋的恐怖片女主角。

In an interview for Filmmaker Magazine, Schoenbrun told Gregg Araki, "Pre-transition, it wasn't that I was asexual—I had plenty of desire—but having good sex in the wrong body was impossible."

Is it wrong to sexually identify so much with a trashy slasher movie? Is it misogynistic or self-destructive to see yourself as the translucent screen between killer and victim? During their private viewing of Camp Miasma, Kris, like a teacher's pet calling out the answer while another student's hand is up, chirps, "This part's transphobic," to which Billy replies, simply, "Shut up." "Okay," says Kris.

To exorcise a miasma, the Greeks performed purification rituals. These rituals might involve inflicting fatal vengeance upon the original transgressor. Sometimes, priests washed the miasma away using water or the blood of a sacrificial animal. In Teenage Sex and Death, a miasma of hyper-intellectual anxiety clouds Kris's mind, preventing her from reaching sexual ecstasy. To purge the curse, Billy invokes Little Death to hack through the figures who embody Kris's hangups—the annoying polycule, the obnoxious movie studio executives, the money-grubbing agent, and, finally, Kris's biggest enemy, herself—and baptize her in blood. "It's all just flesh and fluids," Billy says, against Kris's endless yapping about the gender dynamics of the slasher genre.

Is it toxic to fantasize about watching your partner's partner get killed just so you can cum? Is it disempowering to shut your brain off so you can get laid? Is the age gap between Billy and Kris problematic? Is Kris selfish for enlisting Billy to sexually heal her through violence? The film seems to dare us to pick apart these questions endlessly in thinkpieces and video essays and on social media. This is the hard part: shut up. Okay?

Slasher movies started running out of steam as the 1980s turned into the '90s. You can only kill so many horny teens before the audience gets bored. But as the trend started to go stale, critics, who had previously dismissed the genre as low-brow misogynistic trash, began to reappraise horror movies as commentaries on misogyny rather than expressions of misogyny. In her 1992 book Men, Women and Chain Saws: Gender in the Modern Horror Film, Carol J. Glover argued that horror movies were actually about women fighting against the oppressive male gaze (represented as the killer point-of-view cam in slashers like Halloween and Black Christmas) and taking back their agency from male oppressors. This is the book that coined the term "final girl," referring to the horror movie heroine who survives the slaughter and defeats the bad guy.

Filmmakers paid attention. Wes Craven's New Nightmare (1994) deconstructed the earlier Nightmare on Elm Street films with a meta story about Freddy Krueger entering the real world to attack Heather Langenkamp, the actress who played the final girl in the first Nightmare movie. Two years later, Craven pounded the last nail into the coffin with Scream. 显而易见，借由《瘴气营地的青春性事与死亡》，申布伦试图将他们对恐怖片执行其主题时那种荒诞、往往相当直白的喜爱，与他们自身对智识与分析的癖好结合在一起。这一切都很好，并且按理说足以成就一部非常出色的电影——假如在所有这些点子的核心处，真有一部电影的话。但人物塑造太过薄弱，世界观也只算中等程度地有质感。那些本可把影片充实成真正有料之物的叙事元素，被弃置一旁，让位给更多的隐喻，宛若一家披萨店用光了酱料，便往饼上多撒些奶酪，指望顾客不会注意到缺了一块要害。就连那寥寥几处精美的手绘实体背景，似乎也满载着隐含的寓言。它们半奇幻的风格，几乎让人想起托马斯·金凯德（Thomas Kinkade）的作品。Kinkade。Kink-aid。比利的援助之手，助长克里丝的 kink（癖好）。小死亡。欲望。坦白说，这一切既太多，又根本不够。

影片开头不久，比利反驳了克里丝对恐怖片那种过度复杂的看法。"恐怖就是血肉与体液而已，"比利告诉她。而若有人假定此处万物皆有更深层含义——我敢打赌申布伦正是在恳求观众这样做——这恰是说明《瘴气营地的青春性事与死亡》如何跌跤的绝佳方式。这部电影的血肉便是它的身体，由暧昧不清的点子构成。当那具身体被戳、被捅、被切开时，松散的隐喻与半成品的寓言喷涌而出，体液将观众的衣衫染成血红。影片的身体被抽干——成了一具无生命的空壳。它那作为生命之血的点子，如今成了观众的责任。

尽管其中某些点子或许能引发深切的共鸣——因而具有个人意义——但这并不会使那些观察、或它们在申布伦这部孱弱电影里的执行本身变得深刻。与申布伦大多数死忠粉丝似乎所认为的相反，关于艺术的分歧并不会抵消任何一方的感受。但一如它的创造者，申布伦的电影断言媒介可以是个人身份认同的核心构成。《瘴气营地的青春性事与死亡》鼓励影片的粉丝用"你没看懂"来回击其批评者，尽管这部电影其实极易理解。他们想要保护申布伦的电影，使之免受它自身所鼓励的那种分析——那种深入、细颗粒的、会让所有潜文本分崩离析的分析。登上通往真正深刻的列车、却在"小死亡"一站下车的问题在于：若你在那里逗留得够久，便会开始忘记，生命与电影之中，远不止性与死亡。

Subtitle: Jane Schoenbrun's third film honors and analyzes slashers — but skips the thesis

There comes a time in every English-speaker's life when they learn the meaning of the French phrase "la petite mort," an adage that, translated literally, means "the little death," and typically refers to the sensation immediately following an orgasm. Given the phrase's innate poeticism, it's often used by scholars, authors, casanovas and perhaps overly didactic filmmakers to convey the fleeting intensity of life's most burning joys. But most obnoxiously, "la petite mort" is a favorite of black-coffee-sipping, cigarette-bumming, beret-wearing young adults, eager to remind you that the phrase can also allude to a part of oneself, deep inside, that has died — snuffed out by the pain of existence. "Don't you see?" they ask between puffs of their Parliaments. "Everything has a dual meaning."

Uh oh. See, it's not that "la petite mort" isn't a beautiful, lyrical phrase on its own; it's just that its novelty wears off quickly. It's not exactly the kind of metaphor you want to hang an entire film on, lest its relative simplicity give way under the heft of a feature film's many parts. Then again, unflinching and sometimes self-important intellectualism is a major aspect of Schoenbrun's whole oeuvre.

At every turn, Schoenbrun buries the movie alive, heaping layer upon layer of meta subtext onto it. And while excavating those layers isn't exactly a demanding task as a viewer — what with them being so thin and all — at a certain point, one gets tired of digging, knowing that the once-promising film at the bottom has already been reduced to its bones.

In the last few years alone, Schoenbrun has proven themselves one of cinema's most promising new voices, carving out a substantial space for a mainstream trans filmmaker in an industry still dominated by cisgender, heterosexual white dudes with weird haircuts. Their films are strange and gorgeously imagined, like hazy clouds of half-remembered dreams that nip at the back of your mind all afternoon as you try to interpret their meaning — until Schoenbrun shines a spotlight on it for you. The resulting rug-pull is a sensation akin to trying to remember an actor's name. You reassure your friends that you don't need their help; you'll get it with just a second to think. "Don't tell me, don't tell me!" you beg. And then, they tell you anyway with a proud look on their face, eliminating the chance to experience the satisfaction that comes with figuring something out on your own.

The movie finds young queer注酷儿，泛指非异性恋规范的身份与欲望；本片以酷儿视角重读类型片。 filmmaker Kris (Hannah Einbinder) tasked with rebooting the retro-slasher注砍杀片，1980 年代兴盛的恐怖亚类型，以面具杀手连环屠杀青年为特征。 franchise, "Camp Miasma" — a cross between "Friday the 13th" and "Sleepaway Camp" — with a more "woke" voice. Kris tracks down the reclusive final girl from the series' first film, Billy Presley (Gillian Anderson), hoping to convince Billy to star in the reboot. Media-obsessed and identity-focused, Kris' neuroses immediately counter Billy's cool, Southern demeanor as the two women huddle up at Billy's house in the wintry woods, where they soon discover a powerful physical connection forged by the "Camp Miasma" films.

Kris initially refutes their chemistry, pushing it away in favor of her work. But Billy persists, patiently trying to help Kris past the fog of her sexual repression ("la petite mort") and toward sexual satisfaction ("la petite mort"). One little death precludes the next, until the in-universe Little Death comes calling, armed with 10 to 17 more half-sketched metaphors hanging from the sharp tip of his bloody harpoon.

Schoenbrun's messaging is about as subtle as a punch to the face or a spear to the heart. There is, of course, a place for that, too, especially in a film that is a partial send-up of tactless slasher注砍杀片，1980 年代兴盛的恐怖亚类型，以面具杀手连环屠杀青年为特征。 movie tropes. Yet Schoenbrun's observations are presented in the film as though they're opaque, overloaded with smug surrealism and jam-packed with visual stimuli designed to wink and nudge at the otherwise obvious. At every turn, Schoenbrun buries "Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma" alive, heaping layer upon layer of meta subtext onto it, killing it slowly. And while excavating those layers isn't exactly a demanding task as a viewer — what with them being so thin and all — there are so many to parse that, at a certain point, one gets tired of digging, knowing that the once-promising film at the bottom has already been reduced to its bones.

Granted, all of this is part of the point, and that's a major reason why "Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma" is so exasperating. Early in the film, Billy tells Kris that she's approaching her reboot of the in-film "Miasma" franchise "too academically." The audience instantly understands that this is Schoenbrun riffing on their own intellectually minded approach, and that Kris is a self-insert character for the movie's writer-director. Kris explains that she knows the studio hired her solely because she's queer注酷儿，泛指非异性恋规范的身份与欲望；本片以酷儿视角重读类型片。, as only a queer person could revamp an outdated, transphobic slasher franchise without Big Cancel Culture piping in. But what the Hollywood suits don't realize is that Kris has been a "Camp Miasma" obsessive since childhood. She's extremely interested in the ways problematic movies can be reclaimed by the real-life minority figures depicted in them, and what can be gleaned from studying these films' merits.

Kris calls the original "Camp Miasma" film her sexual awakening, but can't exactly put her finger on why. All she knows is that she's fascinated by a look Billy's character has in her eye during a sex scene, and that she hasn't been able to get it out of her mind for her whole life. She wants to explore her pleasure, but there's a mental block keeping her from doing so.

Here, Schoenbrun touches on a very real and very interesting shared experience within the trans community, remarking on the dissonance between embracing one part of yourself, but being at odds with another — in Kris' case, her queer identity versus her practiced sexuality. (This is made somewhat tenuous by casting a cis actor like Einbinder, but I digress.) When you work so hard to come to terms with who you are, and when there is so much sexual violence directed toward you in both real life and media, letting yourself be seen as a sexual creature can be a very frightening experience.

With Billy's help, Kris comes to understand that it's the final girl fantasy that turns her on. She wants to run through the woods, vulnerable and boobs a'bouncing. She craves the helplessness, Little Death's (sigh) phallic instrument pressed against her skin. She wants to give herself over to it, to turn her brain off and release herself into someone's control. But understanding the fantasy and accepting it are two different things, separated by a sea of unwarranted shame.

As was the case with Schoenbrun's previous two films, once a grand idea starts to take shape in unique and compelling ways, reality and fantasy must blur, forcing a new layer of subtext about "the media" as an alluring and edifying, yet potentially harmful, entity to emerge. Schoenbrun depicts extended sequences from the in-universe "Camp Miasma" movie, notably directed by Ray Blanchard, the real-life sexologist whose theories of autogynephilic sexuality claim that identification with a desired sex can make a person wish to be the desired sex.

If horror is "flesh and fluids," then the flesh of this movie is a body comprised of nebulous ideas. When sliced, loose metaphors and half-sketched allegories gush forth, fluids staining the viewer's clothes blood-red. The film's body is drained — an inanimate husk. Its lifeblood of ideas is now the audience's responsibility.

In short: The winking Blanchard reference supports Kris' shame about the "Miasma" movies causing her to see herself in an autogynephilic way — the heterosexual man's fantasy of a woman in peril. But worry not, even a viewer who doesn't know who Blanchard is can surmise this correlation to the bigger picture thanks to Schoenbrun quite literally spelling it out for us. While rewatching one of the "Miasma" sequels, Kris takes out a pen and paper, writes the word "DESIRE" in all caps, and underlines it twice.

It's clear that, with "Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma," Schoenbrun seeks to marry their affection for absurd, often quite obvious ways that horror movies execute their themes with their own penchant for the intellectual and analytical. All that is great, and would conceivably make for a very great film — if there were any film at the core of all of these ideas. But the characterizations are too flimsy, the world only moderately textured. Narrative elements that could round out the film into something truly meaty are left on the floor in favor of more metaphors, like a pizza shop running out of sauce and throwing more cheese onto the pie, hoping a customer won't notice an integral piece is missing. Even the handful of beautiful, hand-painted practical backgrounds seem to be laden with implied allegory. Their semi-fantastical style almost recalls the works of Thomas Kinkade. Kinkade. Kink-aid. Billy's helping hand, aiding Kris' kinks. Little Death. Desire. It's all, frankly, a lot and not enough at all.

Early in the film, Billy refutes Kris' overcomplicated idea of horror films. "Horror is just flesh and fluids," Billy tells her. And if one were to assume everything here has deeper meaning, as I'd wager Schoenbrun is begging their audience to do, this is a perfect way to illustrate how "Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma" stumbles. The flesh of this movie is its body, comprised of nebulous ideas. When that body is poked, prodded and sliced, loose metaphors and half-sketched allegories gush forth, fluids staining the viewer's clothes blood-red. The film's body is drained — an inanimate husk. Its lifeblood of ideas is now the audience's responsibility.

While some of these ideas may be profoundly relatable — and thus, personally meaningful — that doesn't make the observations or their executions within Schoenbrun's feeble film profound in and of themselves. Unlike most of Schoenbrun's diehard fans seem to think, a difference of opinion about art doesn't negate either person's feelings. But like its creator, Schoenbrun's film asserts that media can be integral to personal identity. "Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma" encourages fans of the film to fire back at its detractors with "you didn't get it," even though the film is incredibly easy to understand. They want to protect Schoenbrun's film from the very analysis it's encouraging, the deep, granular kind that causes all that subtext to fall apart. The problem with boarding the train to true profundity and disembarking at "la petite mort" is that, if you linger there long enough, you start to forget that there's much more to life and movies than sex and death.
